Strata Complaint Advisor

Live demoGitHub

By Lee Yih Ven · Published 17 November 2026

Designed for the management office of Amber Residences, a condominium of four blocks and 608 units under one management corporation. The admin officer receives complaints about noise, parking, leaks, renovation and charges, and each one turns on the Strata Management Act, its Regulations, the building's by-laws and its house rules. A wrong citation, a fine nobody approved, or a neighbour blamed before anyone checked can turn a complaint into a dispute.

I built an advisor the officer opens before acting on a complaint: the AI finds the rules that apply and drafts what to ask, what to do and what to reply. Code masks MyKad and phone numbers and checks every draft before the officer sees it. The advisor never speaks to residents.

The officer edits and sends every reply, and the building manager decides fines, payments and disputes. In testing, the advisor passed 19 of 20 sample complaints in both runs, covering answers from the documents, handovers and an emergency, and passed the remaining complaint in a third run. One Complaint, From the Screen to the Reply

One complaint from the officer's screen to the reply: loud music after 11 p.m. The branch below shows an emergency from another complaint. Each search and the check sending a draft back are left out here; see the Technical tab and the screen's Under the hood tab.

  1. The admin officer opens the complaint on the screen. Code masks MyKad and phone numbers and checks for emergency words. An emergency goes straight to the building manager, with the note written in code and no AI call.
  2. The AI advisor searches the building's documents and the law above them, in order of precedence. It drafts the guidance: Incident · Check · Act · Reply · Escalate.
  3. Code checks every citation and quote, any fine or payment amount, any breach wording and the handover reason before the officer sees the guidance.
  4. The officer reads the guidance, opens any rule it cites, edits the reply and sends it through the office's own channels. Any site check or notice is the officer's call; fines, payments and disputes go to the building manager.

What It Does for the Management Office

The rule that governs, cited from the documents

The advisor searches the building's documents and the law above them in order of precedence: the Act, then the Regulations and their by-laws, then the building's own by-laws, then the house rules. Where two documents differ it says which one governs, as when the building's sinking fund rate falls below the Act's minimum of ten per cent of the charges. Every citation opens its passage, with its page and the date it took effect.

A report, never a verdict

A complaint is treated as a report until staff have checked it, so the advisor asks for the facts still missing and the reply never names the reported unit. Code sends back any guidance that says a rule was broken while facts are missing. It also sends back a promised fine or payment, and any fine or payment amount that no rule retrieved for this complaint states.

Five reasons to hand over, and an emergency first

The advisor hands a complaint to the building manager for five reasons only: a fine the documents do not support, money or compensation, a legal dispute, an emergency, or a complaint about the management. Every handover note is assembled in code, with the facts collected, the facts missing, the rules cited and the steps already suggested.

Tools, Resources and Workflow

How the AI works

Before any AI call, the loop reads the complaint's intake record; an emergency ends there with the note written in code. Otherwise the advisor gets the complaint and the building's complaint SOP, searches the documents, and submits its guidance. The loop ends when the guidance is recorded, when the check rejects it after two retries, or at 16 turns.

Code Walkthrough

Precedence: the Document That Governs Ranks First

Passages on the complaint's topic are ordered by precedence, then by relevance. Statute text scores low with the reranker, so the score gates only passages off the topic.

# engine/retrieval.py: search, the last stage
        for c in cands:
            c["on_topic"] = complaint_type in c["topics"]
            c["precedence"] = PRECEDENCE[c["tier"]]
        # Statute text scores low on this cross-encoder even when it is the answer, so the score
        # gates only passages off the complaint's topic; a passage on the topic stays a candidate.
        on = [c for c in cands if c["on_topic"]]
        off = sorted([c for c in cands if not c["on_topic"] and c["rerank_score"] >= min_rerank],
                     key=lambda c: -c["rerank_score"])[:max(2, keep - len(on))]
        cands = on + off
        # precedence applies only among passages on the complaint's topic; the rest follow on relevance
        cands.sort(key=lambda c: (not c["on_topic"], c["precedence"] if c["on_topic"] else 0, -c["rerank_score"]))
        return cands[:k]

Before Any AI Call: Masking and the Emergency Check

Fixed words mean danger now. A match hands the complaint over with no AI call; numbers are masked before anything else sees the text.

# engine/intake.py
EMERGENCY = [
    (re.compile(r"\bgushing\b", re.I), "water gushing"),
    (re.compile(r"\bflood(?:ing|ed)?\b(?!\s+(?:risk|insurance))", re.I), "flooding"),
    (re.compile(r"\bburst(?:ing)?\s+(?:water\s+)?pipe", re.I), "burst pipe"),
    (re.compile(r"\bfire\b(?!\s*(?:escape|exit|door|extinguisher|hose|route|alarm test|drill))", re.I), "fire"),
    (re.compile(r"\bsmoke alarm\b", re.I), "smoke alarm"),
    (re.compile(r"\b(?:trapped|stuck)\s+in\s+(?:the\s+)?lift\b|\blift entrapment\b", re.I), "lift entrapment"),
    (re.compile(r"\bcollaps(?:e|ed|ing)\b", re.I), "collapse"),
    (re.compile(r"\bgas leak", re.I), "gas leak"),
    (re.compile(r"\belectric(?:al)? shock\b|\bsparking\b", re.I), "electrical danger"),
]


def mask(text):
    t = MYKAD.sub(lambda m: f"******-**-{m.group(3)}", str(text or ""))
    t = PHONE.sub(lambda m: f"***-*** {m.group(1)}", t)
    return t


def has_personal_numbers(text):
    return bool(MYKAD.search(str(text or "")) or PHONE.search(str(text or "")))

Two Retries, Then the Building Manager

The guidance is recorded only when the check passes, with the office's sign-off added in code. A third rejection shows no guidance and tells the officer to refer the complaint.

# mcp_server.py: submit_guidance
    def submit_guidance(self, cid, guidance):
        def run():
            s = self.session(cid)
            if s.attempts > guard.MAX_RETRIES:
                return {"status": "rejected", "error": "retries_exhausted", "message": EXHAUSTED}
            faults = guard.check(guidance, s, complaint_file.raw(cid))
            if not faults:
                signed = dict(guidance, draft_reply=guard.sign(guidance["draft_reply"]))
                s.guidance = signed            # a follow-up resubmits the whole guidance, checked again
                s.attempts = 0
                s.note = complaint_file.handover_note(cid, signed, s.retrieved) if signed.get("handover") else None
                return {"status": "recorded", "data": {"handover_note": s.note, "draft_reply": signed["draft_reply"]},
                        "next_step": "Guidance recorded for the officer."}
            s.attempts += 1
            if s.attempts > guard.MAX_RETRIES:
                return {"status": "rejected", "error": "retries_exhausted", "faults": faults, "message": EXHAUSTED}
            return {"status": "rejected", "error": faults[0]["error"], "faults": faults,
                    "retries_left": guard.MAX_RETRIES - s.attempts + 1,
                    "next_step": "Fix each line named and submit again."}
        return self._wrap(cid, "submit_guidance", {"guidance": guidance}, run)

  • Expected results: the answer key holds 20 complaints, 15 answered from the documents and 5 handed over, written before the build. An independent reviewer checked every expected result in a separate context, and each change it raised was decided and recorded before the runs.
  • Scored from the record: the tools called, the guidance recorded by the check, the complaint type, the rules cited (each requirement met by any of its codes), no forbidden rule, the handover reason, missing facts named, a conflict resolved by precedence, no personal number in the log, and no model call on the emergency. The wording of the reply is not scored; it was read by a person on a three-case sample before the runs.
  • The 2 + 1 rule: all 20 complaints ran twice on Claude Haiku 5.5, and a case passes only if it passes both runs; 19 did. The one that did not, H2 (a compensation claim), cited by-law BL-25 in run 2, which its answer key rules out. A third run covers only the cases that failed in either: H2, which passed. A case still failing after three runs is reported for a decision, not rerun.
  • Cost: the two runs cost USD 0.12 and the third run USD 0.0023; every run on the project, development runs included, came to USD 0.31.
  • No-key tests: 54 checks: 42 in test_tools.py and 12 in test_mcp.py, all passing; the benchmark runs the 42 tool tests first and refuses a paid run if one fails.

The code that decides how the advisor behaves, cut directly from the project files. The AI reads the complaint, chooses what to search and drafts the guidance; intake, retrieval and the check decide what reaches the officer. Page markup is left out. View the code on GitHub.

projects/strata-complaint-advisor/
├── corpus/legal/              # Act sections, Regulations and Third Schedule by-laws, one file each, with page
├── corpus/building/           # Additional by-laws, house rules, complaint SOP, reply templates (sample)
├── data/                      # chunks.json, the unit register (608 units), the 20 sample complaints
├── rules/                     # SOP steps, handover reasons, facts to collect by complaint type
├── engine/
│   ├── intake.py              # MyKad and phone masking; emergency words
│   ├── retrieval.py           # Vector and keyword pools, rerank, precedence
│   ├── rule_search.py         # Search scoped to the building, in force on the complaint date
│   ├── guard.py               # The check on the guidance before the officer sees it
│   └── complaint_file.py      # The complaint as read, the unit context, the handover notes
├── mcp_server.py              # MCP server: 4 resources, 4 tools, a log per complaint
├── prompt.py, agent.py        # The advisor's prompt and the tool loop
├── serve.py                   # Live mode on your own machine, with your own key
├── build/                     # Extractor, chunker, unit register, screen and answer key builders
├── benchmark/                 # cases.json, run_benchmark.py, test_tools.py, test_mcp.py, runs/
├── demo.html                  # The advisor screen
├── documents.html             # The building documents
└── answer-key.html            # The expected results
